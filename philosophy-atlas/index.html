Book in the making · working draft

A History of Philosophy in the Making

Written gradually from the atlas corpus, by its curator and its contributors.

Preface

It is not only that most women philosophers have been buried by history. Even those whose writings have survived are ignored.

Most histories of philosophy tell one story: a line from Greece through Rome and Christian Europe to modern Europe and North America, carried by a few great names, almost all of them men. This book starts from a different place. It is written from the atlas, a corpus that sets the traditions side by side—Greek, Indian, Chinese, Japanese and Korean, Persian and Islamic, Jewish, European, African and Africana, Latin American—and admits thinkers by their work, not by how much they were later read.

Two things follow. First, the history becomes a history of conversations: who answered whom, where answers converge across traditions that never met, and where old ideas return in new words. Second, the silences become visible. In the corpus as it stands, of the philosophers are women, and most of them have no recorded reply from anyone. That figure does not tell us how many women philosophers there were; it tells us whose works were copied, kept and catalogued, and whose were not. Part of that is history; part of it is the way histories have been written. The book tries to tell both.

The chapters below follow the eras of the timeline. Each one lists the thinkers the corpus already holds, so the outline grows as the corpus grows. Sections are drafted one by one; every claim points back to a text and a reference in the atlas.
